==============================================================================
HOUSE STYLE -- delete this block when the sheet is finished
==============================================================================
Actions only. The decision belongs here; the discussion that produced it
does not. No rationale, no history, no meeting notes.

No dates. Git and the file timestamps carry them. A dated measurement reads
as fresh and invites trust -- an undated one invites re-derivation, which is
what you actually want. Where a figure could go stale, write the instruction
to re-derive it, not the day it was taken.

Name a greppable symbol, never a line number.

Steps are vertical slices: extract the piece, rewire every caller, delete the
original, all inside one step. Never move in one step and rewire in the next.
Every step leaves a working app, so work can stop at any boundary.

Verification lives in the step whose work it proves. A step whose work is not
observable on its own moves its gesture down to the step where it becomes
observable, physically -- never a "deferred, see step N" pointer.

Temporary test hooks are either harmless if left in permanently, or removed
in the step that stops needing them. Never parked in a cleanup step that may
never be reached.

A check belongs at a step boundary only when failing it means stop and fix
before continuing. A check whose failure means "note it and decide later" is
a decision queue, not a gate -- it runs at CLEANUP and at commit, where there
is time to think. Test any new gate against this before adding it.

SCALING -- a two-step sheet should cost two minutes to write:
  GOVERNING RULE  omit when there isn't one. On an ON THE FLY sheet it often
                  collapses into WHY THIS SHAPE -- never write both saying
                  the same thing.
  DOC SYNC        fold into CLEANUP on a sheet of three steps or fewer.
  UNVERIFIED      never omitted. Nothing unverified is itself a claim, so
                  write that one line rather than deleting the block.

WHY THIS SHAPE's four-line cap is the plan/no-plan test: run out of room and
the work needed a plan. It measures explanation, not risk -- a change that
explains in one line can still touch thirty call sites. The second signal is
steps that cannot be stated as vertical slices each leaving a working app.
Neither one alone is enough.
